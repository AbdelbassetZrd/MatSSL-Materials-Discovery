\section{Results Comparison on Stability}

Thermodynamic stability (Alexandria, \StabN{} compositions, \StabPos{}\% within
0.05\,eV/atom of the convex hull) is the largest and the hardest of the three SSL tasks. The
protocol is the same: 1,000 labels and 3 seeds; the test set is capped at 20,000 held-out
compositions.

\subsection*{Comparison of SSL Methods}

\resfig{\textwidth}{results/stability/indist/stability_methods_comparison_balanced.png}{Comparison
of SSL methods on the Stability dataset (unlabeled pool: same database, labels hidden).}

\maybeinput{table_stability_indist}
\maybeinput{table_stability_gnome}

Stability gives the largest semi-supervised gain of the project. With unlabeled compositions
from the same database, \StabInBestMethod{} (\texttt{\StabInBestConfig}) reaches
\StabInBestComp{} against \StabInBaseComp{} for the tuned \StabInBaseConfig{} baseline
(\StabInImpr\%). It is better for each of the three seeds (+0.011, +0.001 and +0.003) and
improves both the F1-score (0.583 against 0.579) and the ROC-AUC (0.794 against 0.788). The
two descriptor views of Co-Training (elemental properties versus stoichiometry and valence
electrons) are designed to make different errors, so that each view can teach the other. Self-Training and
Cluster-then-Label are tied with the baseline.

With the GNoME pool, the gain shrinks to \StabGnImpr\% (\StabGnBestMethod{},
\StabGnBestComp{}), within one standard deviation. In both settings Label Propagation and Label
Spreading stay below 0.60 (ROC-AUC $\approx$ 0.70). Compositions with the same elements can be
stable or unstable depending on their stoichiometry and structure. As a result, neighbouring
points in descriptor space often carry different labels, and propagating labels along the
graph fails.

\subsection*{Effect of the Number of Unlabeled Compositions}

\resfig{\textwidth}{results/stability/indist/stability_learning_curves.png}{Composite score and
F1-score versus the number of unlabeled compositions (stability, same-database pool).}

\maybeinput{poolsize_stability_gnome}

The degradation with the pool size is strongest on stability. With the same-database pool,
Co-Training falls from 0.689 with 1,000 unlabeled compositions to 0.588 with 100,000. With
GNoME it falls from 0.684 to 0.525, and Self-Training from 0.682 to 0.615. A likely
explanation is that GNoME was built from compositions the generative search predicted to be
stable, so its distribution is very different from Alexandria's. As the pool grows, the
pseudo-labels on these compositions increasingly outweigh the 1,000 true labels.

With the same-database pool, Co-Training is above the baseline from 500 to 2,500 labels
(+0.017 at 500, +0.005 at 1,000 and +0.007 at 2,500), and the curves cross at 5,000 labels.
This is the range where semi-supervised learning is expected to help: enough labels for
reliable first predictions, but not enough for the supervised model to see every chemistry.
With the GNoME pool, the pseudo-labels hurt at 250 labels (0.570 against 0.603). The baseline
gains 0.13 composite points between 250 and 5,000 labels. The fully supervised model trained on
all 349,432 training labels reaches \StabInFullComp{} (ROC-AUC \StabInFullAUC{}). Even with
every label, composition alone cannot decide stability, which also depends on the crystal
structure and on the competing phases.

\subsection*{Selected Model}

Both unlabeled-pool settings select the same configuration, so they give the same deployed
model: \StabSelMethod{} re-trained on all training labels with 1,000 GNoME compositions as
unlabeled data. On the held-out test set it reaches a composite score of \StabSelComp{}
(ROC-AUC \StabSelAUC{}, F1 \StabSelFone{}, recall \StabSelRec{}).

Consequently, \textbf{\StabSelMethod{}} (\texttt{\StabSelConfig}) is selected as the primary
architecture for the deployment phase of the stability prediction module.
