\section{Results Comparison on Magnetism}

The magnetism task (JARVIS-DFT, \MagN{} compositions, \MagPos{}\% magnetic) is the most
imbalanced of the three SSL tasks, so the F1-score is lower than the ROC-AUC for every model.
The protocol is the same as for metallicity: 1,000 labels, 3 seeds and \MagInNTest{} held-out
test compositions.

\subsection*{Comparison of SSL Methods}

\resfig{\textwidth}{results/magnetism/indist/magnetism_methods_comparison_balanced.png}{Comparison
of SSL methods on the Magnetism dataset (unlabeled pool: same database, labels hidden).}

\maybeinput{table_magnetism_gnome}

Magnetism gives the second-largest semi-supervised gain of the project: \MagInBestMethod{}
(\texttt{\MagInBestConfig}) reaches \MagInBestComp{} against \MagInBaseComp{} for the tuned
\MagInBaseConfig{} baseline (\MagInImpr\%). The gain is small but present for each
of the three seeds (+0.001, +0.003 and +0.004), on both the F1-score and the ROC-AUC. The
confidently pseudo-labelled magnetic compositions add examples of the minority class.
Co-Training and the graph methods lose 0.08--0.12 composite points: their pseudo-labels follow
the majority class, so the rare magnetic class is diluted.

With GNoME as the unlabeled pool, no SSL method beats the baseline. The best one,
\MagGnBestSSL{} (\texttt{\MagGnBestSSLConfig}), reaches \MagGnBestSSLComp{}
(\MagGnImpr\%), the largest loss of the project. Its best threshold is only 0.75, which
means it accepts more pseudo-labels, on compositions that differ from the labeled data.

\subsection*{Effect of the Number of Unlabeled Compositions}

\resfig{\textwidth}{results/magnetism/indist/magnetism_learning_curves.png}{Composite score and

Self-Training is the only method whose score stays flat (0.79--0.80) from 1,000 to 51,528
unlabeled compositions. With its 0.9 threshold, a larger pool only adds a few more confident
pseudo-labels. Every other method degrades as the pool grows: Cluster-then-Label drops from
0.78 to 0.70 and Co-Training from 0.72 to 0.64. With GNoME, every method, including
Self-Training, stays below the baseline (0.7927) at every pool size.

The label-budget curve shows where SSL is useful: with only 250 or 500 labels, Self-Training
on the same-database pool is about 0.005 above the baseline. The gap closes at 2,500 labels,
because a supervised model then sees enough magnetic compositions on its own. With the GNoME
pool, Self-Training is below the baseline at every budget (by 0.023 with 250 labels). The fully supervised model
(\MagInFullComp{}, ROC-AUC \MagInFullAUC{}) is still 0.10 above any 1,000-label model.
LightGBM is the best supervised family; the tuned version wins by a small margin over the
default one.

\subsection*{Selected Model}

Re-trained on all training labels plus the unlabeled pool, the deployed model reaches a
composite score of \MagSelComp{} on the held-out test set (ROC-AUC \MagSelAUC{}, F1
\MagSelFone{}, recall \MagSelRec{}, precision \MagSelPrec{}).

Consequently, \textbf{\MagSelMethod{}} (\texttt{\MagSelConfig}) is selected as the primary
architecture for the deployment phase of the magnetism prediction module.
