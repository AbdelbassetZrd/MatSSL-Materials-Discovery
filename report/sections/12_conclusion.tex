\section{Conclusion}

This project highlights both the potential and limitations of Semi-Supervised Learning for
materials discovery. Working with only 1,000 labels, more than one million compositions from
five databases and more than 2,600 trained models, we found that SSL can reduce labelling
costs. Its success, however, strongly depends on data characteristics. Self-Training with a
strict confidence threshold was the most reliable method and was deployed for metallicity
and magnetism. \StabSelMethod{} was deployed for stability. Unlabeled data from a different
distribution (GNoME) and larger unlabeled pools tended to hurt rather than help.

Complementing these approaches, our traditional materials-informatics pipeline for the
photovoltaic band gap showed robust performance. A genetic algorithm reduced the 142
descriptors to \GAFinalN{} without loss of ROC-AUC, which makes the model easier to
interpret, and the applicability-domain assessment gives a reliable confidence estimate for
every prediction. The unsupervised (association rules, clustering) and self-supervised (masked
autoencoder) components turned the unlabeled data into interpretable chemical knowledge and a
materials embedding. They are all exposed, together with the four deployed models, in the
MatSSL Studio web application.

Future work will explore hybrid approaches that combine the data efficiency of SSL with the
interpretability of traditional materials informatics, alongside active learning,
structure-aware graph neural networks and richer representations of materials.
